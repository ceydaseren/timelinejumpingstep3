Grieve and Release

Making space in your system for what is coming.

The Teaching

Even when you are genuinely ready to leave something behind, your subconscious and your body may register that change as loss. A pattern you have outgrown, a space you lived in, an identity you wore for years — your system built a relationship with all of it. It does not always distinguish between what was good for you and what was simply familiar. So before you move forward, you give yourself permission to grieve what you are letting go of, even the things you are glad to release.

In this step, you are acknowledging what your system may miss. That could be the identity of someone who struggles, or always falls short, or puts others first. These patterns often became your personality because, at some point, they were trying to protect you. Releasing them means your sense of who you are is also shifting, and that shift deserves to be honored, not rushed past.

This step is important not to skip. At the same time, if nothing comes up for you right now or you are not sure what you would grieve, that is okay too. Something may surface during the Stabilization phase, and when it does, you will have the tools to meet it. Below you will find several ways to support yourself through this process whenever you are ready.

Before You Write

Before you begin, place one hand on your chest and one on your belly. Take three slow breaths, letting your exhale be longer than your inhale. If emotion rises, that is the process working. Let it move.

Your Reflection

Look at what your system may miss. Include patterns, beliefs, relationships, habits, identities — anything your body or subconscious may have leaned on, even if you are glad to let it go.

My system may grieve the loss of...

Describe both sides. Who have you been showing up as? Who is beginning to emerge? Give both of them space here.

The identity I am leaving behind is... and the person I am becoming is...

Ways to Release

Choose what feels right for you. You do not need to use all of these.

Feel it
Allow the emotion to rise without pushing it away. You do not need to analyze it. Your body knows how to move energy when you give it permission.
Cry
If tears come, let them. Suppressing a cry keeps that energy stored in the body. Crying is a release, not a sign that something is wrong.
Speak it out
Go to a mirror and speak to yourself about what you are letting go of. Tell yourself it is safe to release it. Hearing your own voice say the words can shift something that writing alone cannot.
Shake
Shaking is one of the body's natural ways of completing a stress cycle. Stand and let your body shake loosely for a few minutes, without forcing it into a pattern.
Journal and burn
Write out what you are releasing on paper. Then rip it out and throw it away or burn it in a safe place. The physical act signals to your system that you are letting this go intentionally.
Send it to Source
Close your eyes and picture whatever you are releasing gathering into a ball of energy outside your body. Then send it upward to Source energy to be transmuted. You do not need to hold it anymore.
How to Speak to the Part
Use this when a pattern is not shifting through feeling or journaling alone. It works by finding the part of you that is running the pattern and giving it a new role instead of trying to fight or eliminate it.
Step 1 — Identify

Name the feeling or pattern. Example: I keep overcommitting and burning out.

Step 2 — Separate Slightly

Instead of "I am this way," say "a part of me does this." That small shift opens space between you and the pattern.

Step 3 — Get Curious

Ask the part: What are you trying to protect me from? When did I first feel this way? What do you need?

Step 4 — Listen

Quiet your mind, take a few breaths, and notice the first thought, feeling, or image that surfaces. Common answers: if I disappoint people, they will leave. If I slow down, I will lose control.

Step 5 — Acknowledge

Say to this part: thank you for trying to protect me. You do not have to carry this anymore. I am not the same person I was.

Step 6 — Choose a New Role or Release

Ask the part: are you ready to go, or do you want a new role? If it is ready to go, imagine it being transmuted out of your body. If it wants a new role, tell it what you need from it now and wait for its agreement before completing.

Ho'oponopono
Ho'oponopono is a Hawaiian forgiveness practice. This is not about taking blame. We do not always know what is at play on a soul level, but Source does. If something is ready to be cleared, these phrases will clear it. Start by completing the opening line below, naming what you want to release. Then move through the four phrases slowly, repeating them as many times as feels right.
Source, what is it within me that _______________________?
I'm sorry.
Please forgive me.
Thank you.
I love you.
Forgiving Yourself
You can journal it, say the words out loud, or speak them to yourself in the mirror while looking into your own eyes. You do not have to believe the words fully yet. Saying them sets the intention that you are willing to let this go. Allow any emotion that surfaces to move through you.

EFT (Emotional Freedom Technique) works by tapping on specific points on the body while speaking what you are feeling out loud. The act of combining physical touch with voiced emotion helps signal safety to your nervous system, allowing stuck energy to move and releasing the emotional charge around a thought or memory.

Step 1 — Create your set-up statement
While tapping the karate chop point, repeat your set-up statement three times. Use the format: Even though I feel ___, I deeply and completely accept myself.
Step 2 — Tap through the points
Move through points 2 to 9 in order. As you tap each one, speak whatever thoughts are sitting heaviest in you. Say it all. Let the words be raw and honest. You are not performing, you are releasing.
Step 3 — Shift toward support
Once you feel you have gotten it off your chest, begin offering yourself more supportive phrases as you continue tapping. Try: I was doing the best I could. I am always learning. I love myself.
Step 4 — Close at the crown
Aim to finish your final round by tapping on the top of your head. Take a breath. Notice how you feel. Repeat as many rounds as you need until you feel a shift.
